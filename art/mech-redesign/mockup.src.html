Moonsec · player mech redesign · rig mockup

A mech that plants its feet and tracks the cursor

Both concepts below are built from separate pixel-art parts on a runtime skeleton: IK legs that step on the ground, guns that follow the mouse with weight, spring-driven recoil and jump jets. Everything is drawn on a native-resolution pixel grid, the same way it would run in Phaser.

Recommended · Concept A

HARROW — reverse-joint biped

BattleTech-style assault walker: a forward cockpit nose, shoulder missile box, a heavy cannon on the near arm and a gatling on the far arm. Jump jets on the back and both calves.

  • Two independent gun arms, both track the cursor inside a firing arc
  • Reverse knees read as "mech" instantly and keep the silhouette apart from the round drones
  • Jump jets have three nozzles, so thrust reads clearly from any pose

Concept B

BASTION — quad walker tank

A low armoured hull on four spider legs, a domed turret with a long cannon, a chin gatling and a rear launcher. The jets are belly vents for thruster hops.

  • The turret gives the clearest aim read: one big barrel following the mouse
  • Four legs are more forgiving because a trot gait always keeps two feet planted
  • Wider and lower, which suits the tank fantasy but hides more of the floor

How it would ship

Rig, not sprite sheet

Parts authored in Aseprite

One .aseprite file per mech: one layer per part, one slice per pivot and socket (hip, knee, muzzle, nozzle). The CLI exports an atlas plus JSON, and the rig reads its pivots from the slices, so art changes need no code edits.

Solved at runtime

A MechRig owns the parts. A gait controller places the feet, two-bone IK solves the knees, aim trackers turn the guns, and springs handle recoil, landing and hits. Each action is a small state blend rather than a frame range.

One pixel grid

The rig renders into a native-resolution texture that is then drawn at 2×. Rotated parts snap to the same pixel grid as the rest of the game, with no mixed pixel sizes.

Sockets drive gameplay

Bullets, missiles and jet emitters spawn from the rig's live sockets instead of hard-coded offsets like y - 78, so shots come out of the barrel you can see.
